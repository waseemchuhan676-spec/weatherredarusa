🧊 How to Prevent Frozen Pipes

A frozen pipe can burst and flood a home in a single night — and it usually happens in the pipes you never think about: the ones in the attic, the crawl space, the garage, and the outside wall. Learn how to prevent frozen pipes before the first hard freeze, and what to do in the 10 minutes after a freeze warning is issued for your area.

Why Pipes Freeze — and Why They Burst

Pipes can begin to freeze when the temperature outside drops to 32°F (0°C) or below. The pipes most at risk are the ones exposed to cold air: pipes in unheated attics, crawl spaces, basements and garages, pipes running along outside walls, and outdoor spigots. The risk rises sharply when cold lasts overnight or for several days in a row, and when wind drives cold air through gaps around the pipes.

What bursts a pipe is not the ice itself — it is the pressure. Water expands as it freezes, and the trapped water between the ice blockage and a closed faucet has nowhere to go. Pressure builds until the pipe splits, often at a weak joint or a spot you cannot see. That is why a burst pipe is usually discovered as a flood, not as a frozen tap: the break happens while the pipe is frozen, and the water pours out once it thaws.

Do not wait for a pipe to freeze before acting. Prevention costs a few dollars of foam insulation and 10 minutes on the evening of a freeze warning. A burst pipe costs a plumber, a water-damage repair, and often an insurance claim.

Before Winter: Protect Your Pipes

1. Find your main water shut-off valve

Before anything else, find the valve that shuts off water to your whole house — usually near the water meter, in a basement, garage, or utility closet. Make sure it turns. If a pipe bursts in January, this is the first thing you will reach for, and searching for it in a flood is the worst possible time to learn where it is. Tell everyone in the household where it is.

2. Insulate exposed pipes

Foam pipe insulation sleeves slip over pipes and close with a built-in adhesive strip. Cover every pipe you can reach in attics, crawl spaces, garages, and under sinks on outside walls. Pay attention to elbows and joints, which freeze first. In an emergency, wrapping pipes in newspaper or towels is better than nothing. For pipes that freeze again and again even when insulated, UL-listed heat tape or heat cable is an option — follow the manufacturer's instructions closely and never wrap it over itself.

3. Seal the drafts

Cold air leaking onto a pipe is often the real cause of freezing. Seal gaps around pipes where they pass through walls and floors, close crawl-space vents in winter if your home's design allows it, add weatherstripping to garage doors, and insulate walls and attics where pipes run.

4. Handle outdoor water lines

Disconnect and drain garden hoses before the first freeze — a connected hose traps water in the spigot line, and that trapped water freezes and cracks the pipe inside the wall. Close the indoor shut-off valve to outdoor spigots if you have one, then open the outdoor spigot to drain it. Cover outdoor faucets with inexpensive faucet insulators.

During a Cold Snap: 5 Things to Do Tonight

When a Freeze Warning is issued for your area, these five steps take about 10 minutes and cover most of the risk:

  • Keep the heat at 55°F or higher, day and night, even in rooms you do not use. Never turn the heat off completely during a cold snap — a slightly higher heating bill is far cheaper than a burst pipe.
  • Let a faucet drip. A slow, steady drip from a faucet on an exposed line keeps water moving and relieves pressure. Drip the faucet farthest from your main shut-off valve so the water moves through the most pipe.
  • Open cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks on outside walls, so warm air from the room can reach the pipes behind them. Move any harmful cleaners out of the reach of children first.
  • Keep garage doors closed if water supply lines run through the garage.
  • Check on vacant homes. If a neighbor is away, a quick check that their heat is on can save them from returning to a flooded house.

What to Do If a Pipe Freezes

If you turn on a faucet and get only a trickle — or nothing at all — a pipe may already be frozen. Stay calm and work through these steps in order:

  • Keep the faucet open. Run both cold and hot water through it. The flow helps relieve pressure and gradually thaws the pipe from the inside out.
  • Warm the pipe gently. A hair dryer, heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water will do it. Start near the faucet and work toward the coldest section of the pipe.
  • Never use an open flame. Do not thaw a pipe with a torch, kerosene heater, or any open flame — it can start a fire, crack the pipe, or cause an electric shock.
  • Shut off the water if a pipe has burst. If water is spraying or pooling, close the main shut-off valve immediately, open faucets to drain the lines, and call a licensed plumber.
  • Call a licensed plumber if you cannot find the frozen section, cannot reach it, or the pipe has cracked or burst.

Winterizing in Texas and the Southeast

Homes in Texas and the Southeast are the least prepared for freezes — and that is exactly why a rare cold snap there causes so many burst pipes. The February 2021 Texas freeze left millions of homes without power and burst pipes across houses that were never built for sustained sub-freezing cold. The same rules apply there, and they matter more: keep the heat at 55°F or higher, insulate any exposed pipe, disconnect hoses and cover outdoor faucets, and know where your main shut-off valve is before the freeze arrives. Act the day a freeze warning is issued, not after the temperature has already dropped — in the South, the first freeze of the season is usually the one that catches people unprepared.

What to Buy Before the Freeze

These are the product categories worth having on hand before the first hard freeze — all available at any hardware store. Buy them in November, not during the cold snap, when shelves empty fast:

  • Foam pipe insulation sleeves — the single most useful item; cheap, quick to install, and they cover attics, crawl spaces, and garages.
  • Outdoor faucet covers — insulated covers that keep cold air off exterior spigots.
  • Heat tape or heat cables — UL-listed, for pipes that freeze repeatedly; follow the manufacturer's instructions and never wrap it over itself.
  • Smart water-leak detectors — place one near the water heater, washing machine, and under sinks; some can shut off the water automatically when they detect a leak.
  • Pipe insulation for elbows and joints — pre-formed pieces for the spots where straight sleeves do not fit.

Frequently Asked Questions: Frozen Pipes

What temperature do pipes freeze at?
Pipes can begin to freeze when the temperature outside drops to 32°F (0°C) or below. Pipes in unheated areas — attics, crawl spaces, garages, and outside walls — are the first to freeze, and the risk rises sharply when cold lasts overnight or for several days.
What temperature should I keep my house at to prevent frozen pipes?
Keep your thermostat at 55°F or higher, day and night, all winter — even when you are away from home. Never turn the heat off completely during a cold snap. A slightly higher heating bill is far cheaper than repairing a burst pipe and the water damage it causes.
Should I let my faucets drip during a freeze?
Yes. During a hard freeze, let a faucet on an exposed line drip slowly — a steady drip, not a stream. Drip the faucet farthest from your main shut-off valve so water keeps moving through the most pipe. Moving water relieves pressure and is much less likely to freeze solid.
How do I thaw a frozen pipe safely?
Keep the faucet open so water can flow as the ice melts, then warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water, starting near the faucet and working toward the coldest section. Never use an open flame, torch, or kerosene heater — it can start a fire or crack the pipe. If you cannot find or reach the frozen section, or the pipe has cracked, shut off the water and call a licensed plumber.
What should I do if a pipe bursts?
Shut off the main water valve immediately — find it before winter so you are not searching for it in a flood. Open faucets to drain the remaining water, turn off the water heater if the system is drained, and call a licensed plumber right away. Move valuables out of the water and take photos of any damage for insurance.
How do I winterize my home in Texas or the Southeast?
Homes in Texas and the Southeast are the least prepared for freezes, which is why a rare cold snap there causes so many burst pipes — the February 2021 Texas freeze is the clearest example. The same rules apply, and they matter more there: keep heat at 55°F or higher, insulate exposed pipes, disconnect garden hoses and cover outdoor faucets, know where your main shut-off valve is, and act the day a freeze warning is issued, not after the temperature drops.
