Frequently Asked Questions: US Regional Radar

Essential questions answered about regional Doppler radar coverage across the United States.

What is a US regional weather radar?
A US regional weather radar is a live Doppler radar map focused on one part of the country, such as the Southeast, the Northeast Corridor, Texas and the Gulf Coast, the Midwest, the Central Plains, or the West Coast. Regional views use the same NOAA NEXRAD data as the national map, zoomed to the storms that matter in that region.
How do I switch between regional radar views?
Use the region buttons on the National Radar Loop page, or open a dedicated regional page: Southeast, Texas & Gulf Coast, Northeast, Midwest & Ohio Valley, West Coast & California, and Central Plains. Each regional page also links to live city radar maps inside that region.
Which regional radar covers the East Coast?
The East Coast is covered by two regional pages: the Northeast page for the Northeast Corridor from Boston to Washington, and the Southeast page for Florida and the Southeast coast. Both track nor'easters, coastal storms, and Atlantic hurricanes moving up the Eastern Seaboard.
