How to read the SPC outlook

The outlook maps the United States into categorical risk levels. The higher the category, the greater the forecast severe-weather threat in that area. A separate tornado probability map shows the chance of a tornado within 25 miles of any point inside each outlined contour.

CategoryWhat it means
MRGL MarginalIsolated severe storms possible. Stay aware, but widespread severe weather is unlikely.
SLGT SlightScattered severe storms expected. Damaging wind, hail or an isolated tornado is possible.
ENH EnhancedNumerous severe storms expected, with a greater chance of tornadoes, damaging wind or large hail.
MDT ModerateWidespread severe storms likely, with a significant tornado threat in the outlined area.
HIGH HighA major severe weather outbreak is expected. Take every warning in this area extremely seriously.

Outlook vs warning — do not confuse them

An outlook is a forecast of potential: it says severe weather is possible in an area over the coming day. A Tornado Watch means conditions are favorable right now, and a Tornado Warning means a tornado is happening or imminent. A Slight risk does not mean a tornado will happen, and a Marginal risk does not mean you are safe — tornadoes can still form outside the highlighted areas. When a warning is issued for your location, take shelter immediately, whatever the outlook said.

How to Read Tornado Rotation on Doppler Radar

Doppler radar does more than show rain. Its velocity product measures wind moving toward and away from the radar site, and that is how forecasters spot a possible tornado. Look for a tight couplet: a patch of strong inbound wind (usually shown in green) sitting right next to a patch of strong outbound wind (usually shown in red). When those two colors are paired closely together and the couplet persists over several radar scans, it is a classic rotation signature.

Rotation alone is not proof of a tornado on the ground. The National Weather Service confirms tornadoes through a combination of radar signatures, trained spotter reports, and damage surveys after the storm. That is why a radar-indicated tornado warning can be issued before any funnel is seen: the rotation signature is strong enough that forecasters treat it as a real threat.

Tornado Watch vs Tornado Warning

A Tornado Watch means conditions are favorable for tornadoes to develop in and near the watch area. It is a heads-up to review your safety plan and stay near a source of weather information. A Tornado Warning means a tornado has been sighted or indicated by weather radar, and there is imminent danger to life and property. When a warning is issued for your location, take shelter immediately.

What to Do During a Tornado Warning

  • Go to an interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building, away from windows, doors, and outside walls.
  • A basement, storm cellar, or interior bathroom or closet offers the best protection available in most homes.
  • If you are in a vehicle or a mobile home, leave it and seek a sturdy building. Never try to outrun a tornado in a car.
  • Stay away from windows and do not open them. Flying debris, not wind alone, causes most tornado injuries.
  • Stay sheltered until the warning expires or local officials give the all-clear, and keep a battery-powered weather radio or phone alerts on.

Where Tornadoes Are Most Common in the US

Tornadoes can occur in every US state, but they are most frequent in the central and southern Plains and the Southeast, where warm, moist Gulf air regularly meets cooler air and strong wind shear. Spring is the peak season for the Plains, while the Southeast sees a second active period in late autumn and winter. Frequently Asked Questions: Tornado Tracker

What is the difference between a Tornado Watch and a Tornado Warning?
A Tornado Watch means conditions are favorable for tornadoes to develop in and near the watch area, so you should review your safety plan and monitor weather information. A Tornado Warning means a tornado has been sighted or indicated by weather radar and there is imminent danger, so you should take shelter immediately in an interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows.
How does Doppler radar detect a tornado?
Doppler radar measures wind moving toward and away from the radar site. Forecasters look for a velocity couplet, a tight pairing of strong inbound and outbound winds, which indicates rotation in a thunderstorm. A persistent, strong couplet can lead the National Weather Service to issue a radar-indicated tornado warning even before a funnel is reported on the ground.
What should I do if a tornado warning is issued for my area?
Go immediately to an interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building, away from windows, doors, and outside walls. A basement or storm cellar is best. If you are in a vehicle or mobile home, leave it and seek a sturdy building. Stay sheltered until the warning expires and keep phone or weather radio alerts on.
Can tornadoes happen outside Tornado Alley?
Yes. Tornadoes have occurred in every US state. They are most frequent in the central and southern Plains and the Southeast, but they also strike the Midwest, the East Coast, and the West. Any severe thunderstorm in any state can produce a tornado, which is why warnings should always be taken seriously wherever you are.

What is the SPC Day 1 convective outlook?
The SPC Day 1 convective outlook is the Storm Prediction Center's forecast of severe thunderstorm potential for the current convective day, through 12:00 UTC tomorrow. It maps categorical risk levels from Marginal to High and shows tornado, wind and hail probabilities. What does a 5% or 10% tornado probability mean on the SPC outlook?
On a Storm Prediction Center outlook, a 5% tornado probability means there is a 5% chance of a tornado within 25 miles of any point inside the outlined area, and a 10% probability means a 10% chance within 25 miles. Even a 5% tornado contour is a real signal: it is enough for the SPC to highlight the area, so people there should monitor forecasts and have a shelter plan ready. Higher contours such as 15% or 30% mark a much greater tornado threat.
