30-40 dBZ: Moderate Rain
40-50 dBZ: Heavy Rain

How to Use Live Weather Radar in the United States

Weather surveillance radar (Doppler WSR-88D) is the primary remote sensing technology used by meteorologists to detect precipitation, storm structure, and tornado rotation across the United States in real time.

Searching by City or 5-Digit ZIP

Enter any US city name or 5-digit ZIP code in the search bar. The interactive radar map automatically pans and zooms into your exact coordinates, while calculating local temperature, real-feel index, humidity, wind gusts, real-time US AQI, sunrise, sunset, and 7-day outlook.

Safer Severe Weather Planning

1. Monitor the 2-Hour Loop

Look at precipitation movement speed and storm trajectory 30 to 90 minutes ahead of outdoor travel.

2. Check Severe Alert Ticker

Whenever strong red/purple storm cores appear, verify if an active NWS Tornado or Severe Thunderstorm Warning is in effect.

3. Review Air Quality (AQI)

Clear skies can still carry high ozone or fine particulates (PM2.5) during summer heat waves or wildfire seasons.

4. Accurate Astronomical Times

Check precise sunrise, solar noon, and sunset calculated for your specific geographical latitude and longitude.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Doppler radar operation, update frequencies, and severe weather warnings.

Doppler radar emits pulses of radio waves to detect precipitation particles like rain, hail, and snow. By analyzing the frequency shift (the Doppler effect) of the reflected waves, meteorologists can determine both the intensity of the storm and whether it is moving toward or away from the radar site.
The interactive radar map receives updated precipitation frames approximately every 5 to 10 minutes from live radar feeds, with a 2-hour historical loop. The national NOAA mosaic image refreshes automatically every 2 minutes.
Simply enter any US city name (e.g. 'Miami', 'Chicago', 'Austin TX') or a 5-digit ZIP code in the search bar above. The website instantly resolves your coordinates, pans the interactive radar map directly to your location, and loads real-time conditions, hourly forecasts, 7-day forecasts, AQI, and UV index.
Radar colors indicate precipitation reflectivity (dBZ). Light green represents light drizzle; dark green and yellow mean moderate rain; orange and red indicate heavy downpours and severe thunderstorms; purple and pink denote extreme hail or intense storm cores; white or blue highlights snow and winter precipitation.
Severe weather alerts are fetched directly from the official National Weather Service (NWS / NOAA) active alerts API. These include Tornado Warnings, Severe Thunderstorm Warnings, Flash Flood Advisories, and Winter Storm Watches across the United States.
The interactive radar map lets you pan, zoom into your specific neighborhood or county, play a 2-hour loop, and toggle satellite layers. The national mosaic is a high-level composite image covering the entire contiguous United States at a single glance.
The US NEXRAD network features over 160 WSR-88D radar installations operated by the National Weather Service. Major installations include New York (KOKX), Chicago (KLOT), Miami (KAMX), Houston (KHGX), Dallas (KFWS), Denver (KFTG), Los Angeles (KSOX), and Seattle (KATX).
