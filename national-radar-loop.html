Understanding the US National Doppler Radar Loop

The national radar loop connects over 160 WSR-88D NEXRAD Doppler radar stations across the contiguous United States into a continuous, high-definition composite loop. By animating past radar reflectivity scans over a 2-hour window, you can determine:

  • Storm Velocity & Vector: How fast rain bands and thunderstorm cells are moving toward your city or county.
  • Cell Intensification: Whether radar echoes are transitioning from green (light rain) to yellow, orange, red (heavy downpours), or purple (severe hail/tornadic supercells).
  • Cold & Warm Fronts: Long narrow squall lines advancing across states from west to east or south to north.
  • Winter Precipitation Boundaries: The freezing rain and snowfall line during major winter storms.

How to Interpret Radar Reflectivity (dBZ)

The standard NEXRAD radar scale measures echo intensity in decibels of reflectivity (dBZ):

15 – 30 dBZ: Light rain, mist, or light snow showers
30 – 40 dBZ: Moderate rain, steady precipitation
40 – 50 dBZ: Heavy rain, ponding on roadways
50 – 60 dBZ: Severe thunderstorm, high wind gusts, possible hail
60+ dBZ: Extreme storm core, large damaging hail, potential tornado
