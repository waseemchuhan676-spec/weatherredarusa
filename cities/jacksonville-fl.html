🌦️ Jacksonville Weather & Live Doppler Radar Guide

Jacksonville is located in Northeast Florida along the Atlantic Coast and the St. Johns River, making it the largest city by land area in the contiguous United States. The city has a humid subtropical climate featuring long, hot, humid summers and mild, sunny winters. Jacksonville's weather is heavily influenced by the Atlantic Ocean sea breeze and the unique northward flow of the St. Johns River. Daily sea-breeze convergence boundaries in the summer produce intense downpours, frequent lightning strikes, and localized urban street flooding across Duval, St. Johns, and Clay counties.

📡 NOAA NEXRAD Station: KJAX (Jacksonville / Jacksonville, FL)

Our live Jacksonville radar displays Level-III NEXRAD radar data from NOAA station KJAX, situated in northern Duval County. Station KJAX operates with S-band dual-polarization Doppler technology, allowing meteorologists to track offshore Atlantic tropical depressions, scan approaching hurricane rainbands, and detect torrential thunderstorm cores.

🎨 How to Read Doppler Reflectivity (dBZ Scale)

Radar reflectivity measures precipitation density in decibels of Z (dBZ). Use this color reference when monitoring approaching storms on the Jacksonville radar:

15-30 dBZ: Light Rain
35-45 dBZ: Moderate Rain
50-55 dBZ: Heavy Storms
60+ dBZ: Hail / Severe

⚠️ Seasonal Severe Storm Threats in Jacksonville

During hurricane season (June through November), Northeast Florida is vulnerable to Atlantic tropical storms and hurricanes that push dangerous storm surge into the St. Johns River basin and beaches. Summer afternoons regularly bring severe pulse thunderstorms with damaging winds and heavy lightning.

Frequently Asked Questions: Jacksonville Weather Radar

Essential questions answered regarding Jacksonville Doppler radar data, NOAA NEXRAD coverage, and severe storm tracking.

How do I check live Doppler weather radar for Jacksonville, FL?
You can track live high-definition Jacksonville weather radar right here on Weather Radar USA. Our interactive Doppler map updates every 5 to 10 minutes with precipitation loops, storm velocity, rain intensity, and official NOAA NWS storm alerts for Jacksonville and Duval County.
Which NOAA NEXRAD radar station covers Jacksonville and Northeast Florida?
The primary National Weather Service Doppler radar serving the Jacksonville metropolitan area is station KJAX, located in northern Duval County, Florida. It operates with dual-polarization S-band technology under the NWS Jacksonville forecast office.
How does the St. Johns River affect storms in Jacksonville?
The wide expanse of the St. Johns River creates a localized river-breeze boundary that can interact with the Atlantic sea breeze, focusing thunderstorm development right over central Jacksonville and causing prolonged localized downpours.
How do I see active hurricane and tropical storm warnings for Duval County?
Active National Weather Service warnings for Jacksonville, Duval, and St. Johns counties are automatically streamed directly above our radar map from the NWS Jacksonville weather forecast office.
